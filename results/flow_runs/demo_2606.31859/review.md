The introduction of Review Residuals in this work builds upon the principle that gating residual connections can lead to improved performance, as demonstrated by [bachlechner2021]. By conditioning the gate on both the current state and the proposed update, Review Residuals offer a novel approach that differentiates itself from prior gated and scaled residuals, aligning with the human-factors principle of independent verification. _[unverified]_ While [bachlechner2021] and [kimi2026] both explore gating mechanisms, their approaches differ: [bachlechner2021] uses a single zero-initialized parameter, whereas Review Residuals employ a learned, input-dependent gate. In contrast to [graves2016] and [banino2021], which focus on adaptive computation time and reasoning capabilities, respectively, Review Residuals aim to improve the reliability of updates in transformer architectures.

The gating mechanism in Review Residuals is designed to scale each update by a learned, input-dependent gate, which is a key distinction from the fixed coefficient of one used in traditional residual connections, as highlighted in [bachlechner2021]. This approach is expected to provide more reliable updates, potentially leading to faster convergence and better test performance, as observed in [bachlechner2021] for Transformers.
